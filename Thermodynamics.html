Introduction

  • By the year of 2025, thermodynamics and statistical mechanics have been awarded 15 Nobel Prizes and to 30 scientists in different research areas:
  • In 1910, Johannes Diderik van der Waals was awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics for discovering the van der Waals equation that describes the states of non-ideal gases and liquids.
  • In 1918, Max Planck was awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics for his work in black-body radiation and the discovery of energy quantization.
  • In 1926, Jean Baptiste Perrin was awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics for his experimental demonstration of Einstein's theory of Brownian motion.
  • In 1962, Lev D. Landau was awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics for his work in quantum statistical mechanics and the theory of superfluid Helium II.
  • In 1968, Lars Onsager was awarded the Nobel Prize in Chemistry for his work in non-equilibrium thermodynamics and microscopic reversibility.
  • In 1972, John Bardeen, Leon N. Cooper, and John R. Schrieffer were awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics for their BCS theory and explaining superconductivity.
  • In 1977, Philip W. Anderson, Sir Nevill F. Mott, and John H. Van Vleck were awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics for their theoretical work in electronic structure of disordered materials.
  • In 1982, Kenneth G. Wilson was awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics for developing the renormalization group theory to solve critical phenomena and phase transition problems.
  • In 1991, Pierre-Gilles de Gennes was awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics for developing statistical mechanics methods to solve phase transition in liquid crystals and polymers.
  • In 1996, David M. Lee, Douglas D. Osheroff, and Robert C. Richardson were awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics for their discovery of superfluidity in Helium III.
  • In 2001, Eric A. Cornell, Wolfgang Ketterle, and Carl E. Wieman were awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics for their realization of Bose-Einstein condensation in dilute gases of alkali atoms.
  • In 2003, Alexei A. Abrikosov, Vitaly L. Ginzburg, and Anthony J. Leggett were awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics for their contributions to the theory of superconductors and superfluids.
  • In 2016, David J. Thouless, F. Duncan M. Haldane, and J. Michael Kosterlitz were awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics for their theoretical work in topological phase transitions.
  • In 2021, Giorgio Parisi shared half of the Nobel Prize in Physics with Syukuro Manabe and Klaus Hasselmann for his discovery of the interplay of disorder and fluctuations in physical systems from atomic to planetary scales.
  • In 2024, John J. Hopfield and Geoffrey E. Hinton were awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics for their contribution in machine learning and artificial neural networks. The same year in 2024, David Baker, Demis Hassabis, and John M. Jumper were awarded the Nobel Prize in Chemistry for their computational protein design models---AlphaFold and RosettaFold.
