Introduction

  • By the year of 2025, 15 Nobel Prizes have been awarded to 30 scientists in the area of thermodynamics & statistical mechanics:
  • 1910, Johannes Diderik van der Waals, Nobel Prize in Physics for discovering the van der Waals equation that describes the states of non-ideal gases and liquids.
  • 1918, Max Planck, Nobel Prize in Physics for his work in black-body radiation and the discovery of energy quantization.
  • 1926, Jean Baptiste Perrin, Nobel Prize in Physics for his experimental demonstration of Einstein's theory of Brownian motion.
  • 1962, Lev D. Landau, Nobel Prize in Physics for his work in quantum statistical mechanics and the theory of superfluid Helium II.
  • 1968, Lars Onsager, Nobel Prize in Chemistry for his work in non-equilibrium thermodynamics and microscopic reversibility.
  • 1972, John Bardeen, Leon N. Cooper, and John R. Schrieffer, Nobel Prize in Physics for their BCS theory and explaining superconductivity.
  • 1977, Philip W. Anderson, Sir Nevill F. Mott, and John H. Van Vleck, Nobel Prize in Physics for their theoretical work in electronic structure of disordered materials.
  • 1982, Kenneth G. Wilson, Nobel Prize in Physics for developing the renormalization group theory to solve critical phenomena and phase transition problems.
  • 1991, Pierre-Gilles de Gennes, Nobel Prize in Physics for developing statistical mechanics methods to solve phase transition in liquid crystals and polymers.
  • 1996, David M. Lee, Douglas D. Osheroff, and Robert C. Richardson, Nobel Prize in Physics for their discovery of superfluidity in Helium III.
  • 2001, Eric A. Cornell, Wolfgang Ketterle, and Carl E. Wieman, Nobel Prize in Physics for their realization of Bose-Einstein condensation in dilute gases of alkali atoms.
  • 2003, Alexei A. Abrikosov, Vitaly L. Ginzburg, and Anthony J. Leggett, Nobel Prize in Physics for their contributions to the theory of superconductors and superfluids.
  • 2016, David J. Thouless, F. Duncan M. Haldane, and J. Michael Kosterlitz, Nobel Prize in Physics for their theoretical work in topological phase transitions.
  • 2021, Giorgio Parisi shared half with (Syukuro Manabe + Klaus Hasselmann), Nobel Prize in Physics for his discovery of the interplay of disorder and fluctuations in physical systems from atomic to planetary scales.
  • 2024, John J. Hopfield and Geoffrey E. Hinton, Nobel Prize in Physics for their contribution in Machine Learning (ML) and Artificial Neural Networks (ANN). The same year, David Baker, Demis Hassabis, and John M. Jumper were awarded the Nobel Prize in Chemistry for their work on computational protein design models—AlphaFold and RosettaFold.
  • ML and AI have shown their capabilities in scientific discovery, what's next?
